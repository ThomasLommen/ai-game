# Self-evolving project ideas — the terrarium itch

*(brainstormed 2026-08-23: 6 lenses x ~6 ideas each, then judged against the builder profile. This is a menu for a NEW project, separate from the game — same larder spirit as docs/ideas/.)*

**The itch:** build something that grows or evolves itself over time, with few inputs — or none. The joy is checking in and finding it changed itself.

## The judge's shortlist

### [91] Playtest Phylum  `(generative-growth)`
- **Why:** Zero new habits: composes their existing headless harness, overnight runs, and morning census into an evolving artifact. Surprise is real (random S-expr trees finding degenerate exploits = free QA), patches-as-mass-extinctions gives the 6-month arc for free since they'll patch anyway, and the S-expression interpreter is a perfect node --test target. First cool moment is literally Monday morning one.
- **Risk:** Between patches the population converges on one exploit and mornings get samey; 6 games/policy is noisy enough that many 'new champions' are just variance — needs more seeds or the coevolving difficulty-knob population to stay alive, and that second population is unproven hand-waving.
- **Combos with:** Warden — the self-balancing economy

### [87] core.dust — dial-up Tierra  `(self-modifying)`
- **Why:** Purest form of the core desire: nobody types anything and the soup changes itself. Hand-writing the ancestor replicator and the ISA is peak from-scratch joy, the phosphor hex-dump IS the sim not a skin, and parasites emerging unprogrammed around week 3 is a documented, near-guaranteed payoff. Merge in SOUP.EXE's repo-cron + nightly naturalist log so history accrues even with no tab open.
- **Risk:** Classic Tierra stagnation: after the parasite/hyper-parasite arc (weeks 3–8) soups famously settle into a stable ecology. The 6-month claim needs Cellblock's cataclysms, drifting physics constants, and multi-arena migration bolted on from day one — vanilla Tierra alone won't stay surprising.
- **Combos with:** Cellblock 64K

### [84] Dead Letter Office  `(llm-world)`
- **Why:** Best LLM idea in the pool because the surprise engine is structural, not vibes: propagation delay + lossy retelling mechanically manufactures dramatic irony, and the rumor-lineage differ makes drift measurable and debuggable. Cheapest drama per token (~$1/mo), the delay scheduler is a deterministic unit-testable core, and a green-phosphor mail reader is the most CRT-native view in the pool. Reading strangers' mail is a genuinely great check-in.
- **Risk:** Haiku-class prose converges to one voice within weeks and the drama flattens — the state never equilibrates but the writing does. Expect monthly persona-prompt gardening; mild, but it's the one hidden chore.

### [81] Tideglass — an intertidal rock face run by real tides  `(data-organism)`
- **Why:** The most honest zero-input idea: the moon and NOAA are the co-authors, forever, keylessly. Zonation banding emerging from 16-byte genomes is a real, checkable ecology result — falsifiable against photos of actual tide pools, which gives check-ins stakes. Small enough to hold fully in the head, and the desiccation/feeding math is exactly the debuggable kernel they enjoy. Spring/neap at a 14-day period matches check-in cadence perfectly.
- **Risk:** Banding emerges in weeks 1–2, then month-to-month change gets subtle; the 6-month payoff waits on rare events (king tide, storm week) that arrive on nature's schedule, not yours. Could feel becalmed by month 3 without the grazer layer landing.
- **Combos with:** Windward — an L-system hillside fed by your local weather

### [78] Red Queen  `(alife)`
- **Why:** The most watchable idea in the pool — a screensaver with a fossil record. Deleting your hand-coded cohesion rule and watching flocking re-evolve is a first-weekend jaw-drop, the tactic-space scatter of two clouds orbiting for months is a genuinely novel viewing instrument, and the spatial hash + headless overnight mode fit their stack exactly.
- **Risk:** 'Provably no equilibrium' is overstated: predator extinction and prey mutational meltdown are the common real outcomes, and getting sustained oscillation instead of a flatline typically takes weeks of parameter nursing — quiet babysitting the pitch doesn't admit.

### [75] Lenia Bestiary  `(alife)`
- **Why:** Novelty search is the one algorithm in the pool structurally built to keep surprising, and discovers-while-you-sleep is exactly their rhythm. Replay-from-genome (repo stores only JSON, browser regrows the creature) is an elegant hack they'll savor, and the hand-rolled FFT is a delicious contained sub-project.
- **Risk:** Compute math doesn't close: 512²×1000 steps×200 candidates on CPU node blows the Actions budget — needs a smaller grid or WASM/WebGL, and even then novelty archives famously fill with variants only the descriptor metric can distinguish. 'Three new species' may look like three identical blobs by month 4.

### [73] The Beacon  `(llm-world)`
- **Why:** Lowest-risk LLM-world: ~70% is city-economy/agent code they've already shipped, the sim/narration boundary is one JSON event log (a perfect debugging seam), and the coverage→policy→events→coverage feedback loop is a real engine, not narration pasted on. Deterministic + seeded means every moral panic is fully reconstructable.
- **Risk:** The paper-governs-town loop can settle into small policy oscillations that read as noise rather than narrative, and the product ('the gap between story and reality') is subtle — it may take deliberate dashboard design to make the divergence visible enough to delight on a 30-second check-in.

## Angles the whole pool missed (spice for any pick)

- Boredom detector / inverted check-in: no idea instruments its own novelty (statistical surprise metric on world state) to page the builder ONLY when something happened — the single best defense against the month-4 plateau every pitch hand-waves past.
- Other humans as perturbation: nothing lets friends or anonymous web visitors nudge the public Pages world (visitors-as-weather, a guestbook that becomes omens) — cheap non-stationarity and a reason the artifact is public at all.
- Sonification: zero audio anywhere. A generative radio station of the sim (soup churn as noise floor, extinctions as chords) fits CRT taste and enables the most passive check-in possible — leaving it on.
- Phone-native interactive evolution: rating/breeding from the lock screen appears only as a Lenia footnote; a proper thumbs-from-bed selection cadence is the lowest-effort input channel for this PWA builder and no idea owns it.
- Durability engineering: half the pool bets months of history on IndexedDB in a pinned tab (tab death, eviction, phone upgrades). A shared replayable event-log + repo-snapshot substrate reusable across all these projects is the real missing infrastructure idea.
- Physical artifact: a daily receipt-printer census or e-ink frame showing the world would make the check-in ritual off-screen — strong fit for someone whose joy is the morning coffee read, unexplored by all 30 ideas.

## Full catalog

### Artificial life & evolution

#### SOUP.EXE — Tierra in your repo
A self-replicating bytecode ecology that lives inside a GitHub repo: Actions runs the soup nightly, commits the mutated world back, and Pages renders the memory map like a CRT core dump. Parasites and hyper-parasites evolve while you sleep; git history is literally the fossil record.

- **Engine of change:** 64KB circular byte soup, ~500 organisms, each a program over ~32 opcodes with Tierra-style template addressing (JMP seeks complementary NOP patterns). Mutation: 1/2000 bit-flip on copy, 1/10k cosmic-ray flip per cycle, rare segment duplication. Selection: reaper queue kills oldest/most-faulting when soup hits 80% full; CPU cycles are the contested resource, allocated by a slicer whose size-exponent k slowly drifts so optimal body size never settles. Anti-plateau engine: parasites that hijack neighbors' copy loops trigger a host/parasite arms race (template scrambling vs re-matching, then hyper-parasites that steal parasite CPU) — the canonical open-ended coevolution result, reproduced on your hardware. State = soup bytes + organism table serialized to one blob, committed per run.
- **What you watch:** Pages viewer loads latest soup.json: canvas memory map, one phosphor-green pixel per byte shaded by opcode, organism boundaries outlined, parasites in red; a phylogeny tree keyed by genome hash with parent links back to the hand-written ancestor. Daily census diff in the commit: '3 new genotypes >1% abundance; 45-byte parasite now 12% of biomass; clade 0x8f2a extinct.' Checkout any commit to replay any epoch.
- **Input cadence:** Zero mandatory. Optional monthly meddling: tweak mutation rate in config, or hand-write an organism into seed.json and watch it fight.
- **Tech:** Vanilla JS VM (~500 lines) that runs both in-browser (live mode, 10k cycles/frame) and under node in a nightly GitHub Actions cron (50M cycles, ~2 min). node --test for per-opcode VM tests. Repo = database, Pages = viewer. No server at all.
- **First weekend:** VM + 80-byte hand-written self-replicator + reaper + bit-flip mutation in a browser tab. By Sunday night the first size-optimized descendants outcompete your ancestor and the memory map is churning — evolution you wrote from scratch, visibly beating your own code.
- **Six months later:** Hundreds of commits of fossil record: parasite epidemics, evolved immunity, hyper-parasites. You branch the repo at epoch 100 and re-run with different physics as alternate timelines. A Claude step in the cron reads the census and writes a nightly naturalist's log you read with coffee.
- **Why this builder:** It IS their overnight-bots-write-a-census rhythm, aimed at alife. Repo-as-world is catnip for a GitHub Pages deployer; the phosphor memory dump is their exact aesthetic; debugging a tiny VM is prime hood-opening.

#### MURKTANK
A persistent creature tank as a PWA: variable-length genomes decode into sensor->neuron->muscle graphs, food blooms drift on Perlin noise, and carnivory emerges when it pays. Check it on your phone; species have names and family trees.

- **Engine of change:** Genome = variable-length list of 8-byte genes [type|src|dst|weight|threshold|...] decoded into a wiring graph: sensors (food gradient L/R, touch, smell of nearest creature's diet gene) -> tanh interneurons -> muscles (thrust, turn, bite, spawn); body genes set size/armor/herbivore-carnivore diet position. Reproduce at energy threshold with 2% point mutation, 0.5% gene duplication/deletion — variable length lets brains grow complexity. Anti-plateau: 2-3 Perlin food fields whose offsets drift over hours (seasons) so the fitness landscape never sits still, and diet is frequency-dependent — carnivory pays only when grazers are dense, producing endless Lotka-Volterra oscillations instead of a winner. Speciation tracked by online clustering on genome edit distance; clusters get generated names and colors.
- **What you watch:** 60fps canvas when the tab is open; stacked per-species population bands; a species family tree; click any creature to see its brain graph with hot neurons pulsing — the debugger is a microscope. Hourly canvas thumbnails to IndexedDB become a scrollable filmstrip of the tank's whole life.
- **Input cadence:** None required — closed tab fast-forwards via cheap headless catch-up on reopen, or run the node overnight mode. Optional god tools (meteor, wall, food ban) when you feel like stirring.
- **Tech:** Vanilla JS + canvas, sim in a web worker, IndexedDB persistence, PWA manifest for phone. Same sim module runs under node for accelerated overnight runs that emit a morning census. GitHub Pages hosted.
- **First weekend:** Continuous 2D world, 200 creatures, gradient sensing, eat/spawn/die, mutation on spawn. By Sunday you watch spiral-swimming grazers get displaced by straight-line sprinters — the first unscripted takeover.
- **Six months later:** A months-long filmstrip; you've seen three separate carnivore emergences and crashes, an armor arms race, and one species that loiters on bloom edges like it's farming. The species-naming log reads like a paleontology journal.
- **Why this builder:** Phone check-ins via PWA, one codebase for both live-watching and overnight census mode, and the brain-graph inspector turns their love of debugging into the core viewing pleasure.

#### Lenia Bestiary
A nightly cron mutates Lenia genomes and a novelty-search curator keeps only genuinely new lifeforms; you wake up to fresh specimens in a growing public zoo. Novelty search is the one algorithm explicitly built to never converge.

- **Engine of change:** Genome = ~40 floats: 3 kernel rings (radius/weight), growth function mu/sigma, dt, plus a small RLE seed pattern. Nightly job mutates archive parents (adaptive gaussian sigma), simulates 512x512 Lenia for 1000 steps in node, computes a behavior descriptor (mass, centroid speed, angular momentum, mass variance, symmetry score), and admits a specimen only if its mean distance to k=15 nearest archive members exceeds threshold — novelty pressure structurally prevents the boring attractor. Bonus loop: pairwise 'ecosystem trials' drop two archived species in one arena and log interactions (orbit, merge, consume) into a relationship graph.
- **What you watch:** A Pages bestiary: grid of living specimens re-simulated in-browser from genome (deterministic, so the repo stores only JSON — the browser regrows the creatures). Each specimen page has genome, discovery date, parent link, behavior stats. Morning digest: '3 new species: a glider that sheds mass, a rotating trefoil, a breather.' Phylogeny of discoveries grows weekly.
- **Input cadence:** Zero. Optional: star-rate specimens to bias parent selection (interactive evolution lite) whenever the mood strikes.
- **Tech:** Node + typed arrays for the cron sim (write your own radix-2 FFT for the convolution — a delicious from-scratch sub-project), GitHub Actions nightly with a 30-60 min budget (~200 candidates), vanilla JS canvas/WebGL replay on Pages, archive committed as JSON.
- **First weekend:** Lenia in a canvas with the Orbium preset plus a mutate button; a manual mutation safari finds 4-5 novel creatures by Sunday. That's enough to feel the pull — then wire the curator.
- **Six months later:** 500+ specimens spanning gliders, oscillators, self-repairing blobs; the who-consumes-whom graph has surprises; you've forked a 'harsh physics' branch world. Scrolling the discovery timeline is scrolling six months of alien taxonomy.
- **Why this builder:** Discovers-while-you-sleep is exactly their rhythm; deterministic replay-from-genome (tiny repo, living site) is the kind of elegant hack they savor; hand-rolled FFT scratches the build-from-scratch itch.

#### Driftwood Archipelago
Eight islands run as parallel GitHub Actions matrix jobs, each with its own drifting climate; rare rafting events carry genomes between them through committed migration files. Allopatric speciation, then invasions when continents reconnect — infrastructure as biogeography.

- **Engine of change:** Shared sim: 64-byte genomes, fixed 12-neuron brains, sexual reproduction with crossover and a mating-compatibility threshold on genome distance. Each island's climate (food richness, metabolic temperature cost, terrain roughness) does a slow random walk plus a global Milankovitch sine (~3-week period) forcing ice ages. Nightly: each matrix job advances its island 100k ticks from committed state; with p=0.15 an island exports 3 genomes to /migrations/, imported next night along a drifting adjacency graph (continental drift reconnects islands every few weeks). Anti-plateau: islands climb different fitness peaks, and migration keeps injecting battle-tested foreign genomes — invasions, hybrid zones, and measurable speciation the moment two reunited populations exceed the compatibility threshold.
- **What you watch:** Pages dashboard: archipelago map with per-island thumbnails and species pies, animated edges showing last night's migrations, a time slider scrubbing the whole committed history, and headline events: 'Islands 3 and 7 no longer inter-fertile (distance 0.42 > 0.35) — SPECIATION EVENT #14.'
- **Input cadence:** None. Occasional meddling: edit one island's climate JSON or hand-carry a migrant genome across the map.
- **Tech:** Node sim, GitHub Actions nightly with strategy.matrix (8 parallel jobs + merge/commit step), vanilla JS dashboard on Pages fetching the repo's JSON history. node --test on the genetics (crossover, compatibility metric).
- **First weekend:** Single-island sim, then run two copies locally with different food richness and diff the evolved genomes — visible divergence on day one. Wire the matrix cron the following weekend.
- **Six months later:** An event log that reads like a saga: 14 speciation events, two invasive-species catastrophes when drift reconnected old neighbors, an ice age that wiped 5 islands and the radiation that refilled them.
- **Why this builder:** Uses GitHub Actions as the planet's physics — the infrastructure-as-ecology conceit will delight someone who already deploys to Pages and lives by nightly cron reports. Discrete nightly ticks match the check-in rhythm perfectly.

#### Red Queen
Murmurations versus hawks where both sides' steering brains evolve continuously and forever. Frequency-dependent selection means tactics cycle — flock, scatter, ring, bait — with provably no equilibrium to converge to.

- **Engine of change:** Prey genome = ~40 floats: weights from sensors (3 nearest neighbors' bearing/distance, nearest predator bearing/closing speed, wall proximity) through 2 tanh hidden units to turn/thrust. Predators same plus a sprint-vs-endurance stamina gene. No generations: death by predation/starvation, birth on energy surplus with sigma=0.05 gaussian mutation. The engine is pure Red Queen dynamics — any dominant prey tactic creates selection pressure for its counter, and vice versa; local plankton regrowth moves grazing pressure around so flocks migrate. Track it: random-project all brain weights to 2D and watch the two population clouds orbit each other instead of settling.
- **What you watch:** A full-screen canvas that is genuinely beautiful — trails, kill flashes, 1-2k agents. Overlays: the tactic-space scatter (two clouds circling each other over weeks is the money shot) and a rolling capture-rate chart that oscillates forever like an EKG. Nightly headless run emits a battle report: capture curve, notable lineages ('predator line 0x3c: 900 kills'), tactic drift summary.
- **Input cadence:** Zero. Rare knob nudges: predator population cap, plankton richness.
- **Tech:** Vanilla JS canvas at 60fps with a hand-built spatial hash grid for neighbor queries; same sim under node for compressed overnight runs; IndexedDB/JSON saves; GitHub Pages. node --test on the physics and hash grid.
- **First weekend:** Boids with a hand-coded predator, then make prey weights evolvable and delete your hard-coded cohesion rule. By Sunday, flocking re-emerges from selection alone — the classic jaw-drop moment, and it's yours from scratch.
- **Six months later:** You've named eras ('The Scatter Dynasty, weeks 3-7'); prey once evolved wall-hugging and predators answered with patrol arcs; the capture-rate EKG spans half a year and still isn't flat.
- **Why this builder:** The most watchable idea on the list — screensaver-grade — with a chartable, censusable artifact (tactic-space drift) underneath. Guaranteed non-convergence means the 6-month horizon is built into the math, not hoped for.

#### Canopy
A 2D vertical garden strip where plant bodies are grown cell-by-cell by evolved gene regulatory networks and the only resource is raycast sunlight. Watch moss give way to grass give way to a canopy height arms race — then a fire resets succession.

- **Engine of change:** Genome = GRN of ~16 genes, each {activation threshold, +/- weighted links to other genes, action: divide-up/left/right, thicken, leaf, root, seed}. Development from one cell on soil: each tick every cell evaluates the GRN on local inputs (light, cell age, distance-from-root morphogen, neighbor contact); actions cost energy; leaves absorb raycast sun whose angle cycles seasonally; shade starves the understory. Anti-plateau: height is frequency-dependent (a tall forest makes shade-tolerance profitable, opening vine/epiphyte niches via an attach-to-neighbor action), seeds need light gaps to germinate (real gap dynamics), storms snap tall stems, and rare fires spread through dry biomass and restart succession — intermediate disturbance keeps diversity permanently.
- **What you watch:** The strip rendered as chunky terminal-green cells, pure CRT. The killer view: a core-sample time-lapse — one pixel column appended per hour — that scrolls for months showing succession bands, fire scars, and the Tall Era like tree rings. Census: biomass by genome-cluster species, canopy height percentiles.
- **Input cadence:** None. God powers when bored: lightning strike, drought slider, plant a hand-written seed genome.
- **Tech:** Vanilla JS canvas PWA; a few thousand cells so the sim is cheap in-tab; same code under node for overnight fast-forward; IndexedDB saves; the core sample is one ever-growing canvas strip. GitHub Pages.
- **First weekend:** Cells + GRN evaluator + light raycast + seed dispersal. By Sunday the first weed that discovered 'grow up twice, then leaf' carpets the world and you can read its 16-gene program like disassembly.
- **Six months later:** The core-sample strip is a wall-poster-worthy stratigraphy of your world: fire scars, the Vine Plague, a shade-dweller radiation. You've bred a bonsai lineage in a side-branch save.
- **Why this builder:** Developmental biology is the one system genre they haven't built yet; ASCII-garden fits the terminal aesthetic exactly; 'why does this gene loop oscillate?' is peak open-the-hood debugging; the core sample is accreted history made into a single image.

### LLM-agent living worlds

#### Smallwick
An 8-agent village that lives one day per real day on a GitHub Actions cron, and the git history IS the world history. Every morning a new issue of the Smallwick Gazette is committed, written by an editor agent who was there.

- **Engine of change:** Mutating state: /agents/{name}/memories.jsonl (append-only, one JSON memory per line with LLM-scored importance 1-10), relationships.json (NxN affinity floats + labeled edges: rival/married/owes-money), and per-agent beliefs appended to persona.md by weekly reflection calls. Retrieval is embeddings-free vanilla JS: score = 0.99^age_days * importance + tag overlap with today's plan, top 12. Selection pressure: a JS-enforced ledger — agents whose balance hits 0 must change occupation, forcing new locations, new co-locations, new memories. No plateau because reflection compresses each week into beliefs that feed the next week's planning prompt — drift compounds — and an almanac injects dice-rolled perturbations (stranger arrives every 10-20 days, drought, festival) that reshuffle the co-location graph.
- **What you watch:** A CRT-newsprint PWA on GitHub Pages: today's Gazette front page, a scrollable archive of every issue, a canvas relationship graph where edges thicken/redden over months, and a 'this day last month' widget. When something in the paper seems off, you open the repo and grep the actual memory that caused it.
- **Input cadence:** Zero mandatory. Optional: drop a file in /events/inbox/ ('a wolf is seen near the mill') and the next tick weaves it in. Realistic touch rate: once a week for fun, once a month to tune prompts.
- **Tech:** Vanilla JS node script (tick.js) run by GitHub Actions cron daily 06:00 UTC; state as JSON/JSONL committed to the repo; claude-haiku-4-5 via Batch API. Per tick: 8 agent-plan calls (~2.5k in/400 out) + ~5 co-location dialogue calls (1.5k/300) + 1 editor call (5k/900) ≈ 33k in / 5.6k out ≈ $0.06/day, ~$0.03 batched; weekly reflections add ~$0.05. Under $1.50/month. Pages renders the site; node --test covers the deterministic resolver.
- **First weekend:** 3 agents, no economy, tick script runs locally. Saturday: memory store + retrieval scoring + one plan call. Sunday night: run 14 ticks in 'time-warp mode' while you sleep (exactly your bot-playtest rhythm) and read 14 gazettes with coffee. The first time two agents independently mention the same argument, you're hooked.
- **Six months later:** ~180 issues, a marriage, two feuds, one agent who slowly became the village's de facto mayor because reflection kept reinforcing 'people listen to me'. Old agents retire and children arrive with LLM-blended personas, so the cast has turned over once. You can git-blame a grudge to the exact tick it started.
- **Why this builder:** It's their overnight-census loop turned into a permanent institution; agents + city-economy systems are literally what they've already built, minus the LLM narrator. GitHub Pages + Actions means zero new infra, and every subsystem (retrieval scorer, ledger, resolver) is a pure-JS unit they can node --test.

#### Dead Letter Office
Six characters in scattered outposts who can only interact by letters that take 2-7 day-ticks to arrive, so everyone acts on stale information. The world is the mailbag, and the drama is dramatic irony.

- **Engine of change:** State: /mail/in-transit.json (letters with delivery dates from map distance), per-agent received-letter archive, and a 600-token-capped LLM-maintained 'correspondence digest' per pen-pal (this is the memory compression — digests drift as relationships do). The engine of surprise is propagation delay + lossy retelling: rumors are JSON objects with lineage IDs; every retelling is a fresh generation from the reteller's digest, so content mutates telephone-game style, and you can diff a rumor against its ancestor. Deals collapse mid-transit, condolences arrive for people who recovered, two agents court the same third party for weeks without knowing. Delay means no equilibrium: the network never has consistent state to settle into.
- **What you watch:** A green-phosphor terminal mail-reader PWA: per-character inboxes, a network map with mail animating along routes (their network-map muscle), a 'steamer arrivals' board, and a weekly Postmaster's Gazette summarizing gossip. Best check-in feel of any idea here — you literally read other people's mail.
- **Input cadence:** None required. Optional: you can post an anonymous letter into the system yourself (a tip, a threat, a fake inheritance notice) and watch it ripple for weeks. Monthly at most.
- **Tech:** GitHub Actions daily cron, vanilla JS, all state committed JSON. 6 letter-writing calls/day (~3k in incl. digests + delivered letters, 500 out) + 1 digest-update call + weekly gazette ≈ 20k in / 3.5k out ≈ $0.037/day, ~$0.02 batched. claude-haiku-4-5. Pages hosts the reader; letters render from the repo at runtime, no build step.
- **First weekend:** 3 agents, fixed 3-tick delay, no rumor lineage yet. Write the mailbag data structure + delivery scheduler Saturday (pure JS, testable), wire one letter call Sunday, time-warp 20 ticks overnight. First payoff: an agent replies to a question that a later (undelivered) letter already answered.
- **Six months later:** A 1000+ letter archive that reads like an epistolary novel nobody wrote; rumor lineages you can trace mutating across 15 retellings; one outpost that went silent for 40 ticks and the others' letters about WHY are all wrong in different ways. The archive is genuinely worth scrolling.
- **Why this builder:** Cheapest-per-drama idea on the list and the most CRT-native (it IS a terminal mail client). The delay scheduler and rumor-lineage differ are exactly the kind of deterministic, unit-testable core they enjoy, with the LLM only at the edges.

#### The Beacon
A deterministic 300-citizen town sim (free, pure JS — their existing wheelhouse) narrated by three LLM reporter agents with beats, memory, and grudges — and the editor's editorials feed back into town policy knobs, so the newspaper actually governs the town it covers.

- **Engine of change:** Two coupled loops. Loop 1 (free): citizens as structs {job, wealth, block}, businesses, poverty-weighted crime dice, elections every 90 ticks — emergent on its own. Loop 2 (LLM): each reporter has a beat-memory file ('third warehouse fire this month') that accumulates narrative frames; the editor's editorial emits a strict-JSON sidecar {policy: 'police_budget', direction: +1, strength} that nudges sim knobs. The feedback loop is the engine: coverage shapes policy shapes events shapes coverage. Reporters develop persistent editorial slants via their memory files, so the SAME sim events get framed differently over time — and wrong framings cause wrong policies cause visible disasters the paper then covers. Citizens write letters-to-editor and remember being covered.
- **What you watch:** A newsprint-on-CRT front page every morning: headlines, three columns, editorial, letters. Plus a stats dashboard (sparklines of crime/wealth/population) so you can see where the paper's story and the town's reality have diverged — that gap is the product.
- **Input cadence:** Zero. Optional lever: you are the publisher — once in a while you can spike a story or force a front page, then watch the policy consequences for weeks.
- **Tech:** Sim runs anywhere (browser tab or the Actions runner) at any speed, costs nothing. LLM layer: 3 reporter calls (4k in event-log slice + beat memory / 600 out) + editor (3k/500) + 1 citizen letter (1.5k/300) ≈ 17k in / 2.6k out ≈ $0.03/day on claude-haiku-4-5, ~$0.015 batched. GitHub Actions cron + Pages. The sim core is node --test heaven.
- **First weekend:** They could port chunks of their existing city-economy code Saturday; Sunday wire one reporter with a 5-issue memory window. First cool moment: issue 4, when the reporter unprompted calls back to issue 1's fire.
- **Six months later:** 180 issues where you can trace a moral panic: petty-theft blip -> reporter's crime frame hardens -> editorials push police budget -> business tax rises -> closures -> real unemployment crisis the paper caused and now covers as mystery. Fully reconstructable from the archive because the sim is deterministic and seeded.
- **Why this builder:** Lowest risk on the list: 70% of it is systems they have already shipped (city economy, agents, census reports). The LLM layer is thin, cheap, and replaceable, and the sim/narration boundary is one JSON event log — perfect debugging seam.

#### The Ministry
Six clerk agents in an infinite bureaucracy whose constitution is an executable rules file — clerks process procedurally generated petitions under rules a JS engine actually enforces, and once a week one clerk drafts an amendment the others vote on. The law code amends itself; Kafka emerges.

- **Engine of change:** The mutating state is rules.json: a tiny condition->effect DSL you write (e.g. {if:'petition.stamps<3', deny:'form_27B'}), enforced by a pure-JS engine — the LLM only chooses among legal moves and writes justification memos. Weekly, a rotating 'reformer' clerk emits an amendment as strict-JSON DSL (engine-validated; invalid = rejected, one retry); clerks vote via cheap calls informed by precedent memories of their own past cases. Selection: the petition backlog is the fitness signal — rule sets that grow the backlog breed reform pressure in the prompts. Emergence: rule interactions produce circular dependencies (form A requires B requires A) which the engine detects and logs as 'Kafka events'. The law code never stabilizes because petitions are procedurally generated to probe whatever the current rules are.
- **What you watch:** Terminal dashboard: the law code as a git diff view (every amendment is a commit), the backlog graph, a memo archive with clerks citing their own precedents, and a Kafka Event Hall of Fame. Reading a clerk's straight-faced memo justifying an absurd denial is the daily joy.
- **Input cadence:** Zero. Optional: file a petition yourself as 'Citizen K.' and follow your case number through the system for weeks.
- **Tech:** GitHub Actions daily cron; DSL engine in vanilla JS, aggressively node --test-ed (that engine is 60% of the build and 100% deterministic). 6 clerk calls (2k/300) daily + weekly amendment (3k/600) + 6 vote calls ≈ 13k in / 2k out ≈ $0.023/day claude-haiku-4-5, ~$0.012 batched. Pages dashboard reads committed JSON.
- **First weekend:** Write the DSL engine + petition generator Saturday (pure JS, seeded PRNG, fully testable — the fun kind of from-scratch). Sunday: 3 clerks, one amendment cycle in time-warp. First Kafka event usually appears within 30 simulated days.
- **Six months later:** A 200-commit constitutional history with eras (the Stamp Reform Period, the Great Backlog), rules nobody remembers the reason for that the engine still enforces, and clerks whose precedent memories make them jurisprudentially distinct. The git log of rules.json is the civilization.

300 agents on a small-world graph evolve a language from nothing via naming games; agents die and children learn imperfectly from limited samples, and that bottleneck is what forces grammar to emerge (Kirby's iterated learning, for real). You read the dictionary diff each morning like a newspaper.

- **Engine of change:** Meanings are structured tuples {shape, color, size, motion}; forms are syllable strings over a fixed phoneme inventory. Agents hold lexicons (meaning -> form, with confidence weights). Each daily generation runs thousands of Steels naming games: speaker utters, hearer guesses, success reinforces, failure laterally inhibits competing forms. 2% of agents die per tick; children learn from a capped sample of parent utterances — the transmission bottleneck that measurably produces compositional structure. Dialects emerge from graph communities; anti-plateau: the meaning space grows (new referents built by combining old ones, forcing compounds and derivation), stochastic sound-change rules (lenition, vowel shifts) drift per community, migrations remix populations, and god-imposed taboos force euphemism treadmills — an attested real-linguistics dynamic.
- **What you watch:** Static Pages site rebuilt each tick: today's dictionary where every word carries its full etymology chain back to its coinage date; an isogloss map (agents plotted, colored by which form they use for a probe meaning); word-family trees; and a daily diff feed ('kramu displaced obek for [large+red+moving] in the northern cluster').
- **Input cadence:** Zero. Occasional god verbs: introduce a new referent, force a migration, declare a word taboo and watch three generations of euphemisms churn.
- **Tech:** Dependency-free node sim on GitHub Actions daily cron (one generation per day; bump to hourly if impatient), state JSON committed to the repo, vanilla JS static viewer on Pages. It's all strings and Maps — nothing to install.
- **First weekend:** Naming game + lexicon reinforcement + population turnover + the dictionary page. Shared vocabulary crystallizes in the very first run; measurable compositionality (shared morphemes for 'red' across words) within a couple weeks of daily generations.
- **Six months later:** An etymological dictionary thousands of entries deep — trace 'skarn' back through 40 sound changes to a day-3 coinage; northern and southern dialects mutually unintelligible; taboo cycles and a growing meaning space keep it from ever freezing. The scrollback IS the product.
- **Why this builder:** Text-first output is peak terminal aesthetic; the whole sim is vanilla-JS data-structure play; and it accretes exactly the kind of history-worth-rereading their census-report ritual proves they love.

#### Metagame Observatory

#### Augur Row
An ecology of tiny forecaster programs that bet daily on real weather, river gauges, and crypto closes; because the real world is non-stationary, the fitness landscape never stops moving. Evolution powered by the actual sky.

- **Engine of change:** Genome = 96 tokens for a small stack VM (~30 ops: lagged-input load, arithmetic, compare, EMA, conditional) over normalized feature streams: Open-Meteo temps/wind for 5 cities, a USGS river gauge, BTC/ETH daily closes, day-of-year sine. Each forecaster holds 'grain'; daily settlement scores yesterday's predictions (absolute/pinball error) and redistributes grain pari-mutuel within each target's betting pool — separate pools per target = niching, so specialists survive. Bankrupt forecasters die; rich ones spawn offspring via point mutation, token indels, crossover. No plateau because the targets themselves shift: seasons turn, weather regimes flip, markets change character — overfit lineages get culled at every regime change, and you add a new data stream monthly as a fresh niche.
- **What you watch:** Leaderboard page: each forecaster's disassembled program, lineage, and equity curve; a births/deaths feed; a population chart where seasonal extinction spikes are visible. The best moment: reading a champion's disassembly and realizing it independently discovered persistence-plus-seasonal-correction.
- **Input cadence:** Zero — data sources are keyless free APIs (Open-Meteo, USGS). God moves when curious: add a data stream, tax a monopolist lineage, fork the population.
- **Tech:** Two GitHub Actions daily crons (fetch features, then settle + breed at 06:00 UTC), dependency-free node script, state JSON + append-only CSV history in the repo, vanilla JS dashboard on Pages.
- **First weekend:** Stack VM + mutation + Open-Meteo fetch + settlement + leaderboard. Within days the population rediscovers persistence forecasting and the first equity-curve drama appears.
- **Six months later:** Lineages that survived two season changes, extinction events aligned with real weather regime shifts you can point to on a weather map, a champion gallery of disassembled strategies. Reality supplies the novelty for free, forever.
- **Why this builder:** Stack-VM disassembly is peak terminal aesthetic and endlessly tinkerable; coupling a sim to real-world data gives check-ins actual stakes; the whole thing is diffable JSON in a repo, tested with node --test.
